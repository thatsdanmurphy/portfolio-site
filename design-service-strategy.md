# Design Service Strategy — Context for New Task

## The Opportunity
Dan is building a separate design service alongside his job-search portfolio. The core offer: a fixed-scope, fixed-cost, fixed-timeline website design and build for founders, independent professionals, small businesses, artists, and makers.

Working name: **The Two-Week Site**

---

## The Two Personas
- **Dan the job-seeker** — portfolio at danmurphy.work, showing 15 years of product design work at Fidelity, Accenture, Motional
- **Dan the builder** — design service targeting people who need a proper home on the internet

These are separate audiences with separate goals. Keeping them on the same site creates cognitive dissonance for both. The service should live at its own URL.

---

## The Offer (current state)
From the pitch doc (`proper-home-on-the-internet_v1.pdf`):

- **Deliverable:** A clean, well-designed website — home, about/story, offering/services, photos/work, contact
- **Timeline:** 2 weeks (Week 1: discovery + first draft / Week 2: feedback + launch)
- **Pricing tiers:**
  - Independent artists / makers — $8,000
  - Local businesses — $10,000
  - Established companies — $14,000
- **Visual direction** (type, color, layout) included. E-commerce / complex systems scoped separately.

---

## What's Working in the Pitch
- The design of the one-pager is itself proof of capability — it looks right
- The two-week framing is concrete and memorable
- "A proper home on the internet" is a strong, plain-language value statement
- The tiered pricing is clear

---

## Feedback Received + Key Strategic Questions

**On value prop:**
Reviewer flagged that "speed" might not be the primary purchase driver. Agreed reframe: the real value is **certainty** — clear cost, clear timeline, clear deliverable. The anti-agency pitch. Speed is a feature; certainty is the value prop.

**On target customer:**
Current pitch implicitly targets people *starting from scratch* (no site yet). But the **refresh market** (people with outdated or embarrassing existing sites) may be larger and easier to sell — they already know they have a problem. The pitch should address both: "whether you're starting from scratch or your current site no longer reflects who you are."

**On social proof:**
The doc says "lately I've been helping founders, small businesses, artists, and makers..." but shows zero examples. Even 2–3 live URLs would change the credibility of this completely. This is the biggest gap right now. **Getting 2–3 reference clients (even at a deal) before a serious push is the right move.**

**On call to action:**
The pitch has contact info but no "here's how this starts." Needs a clear next step — even one sentence.

---

## Positioning Statement (draft to develop)

Based on a framework from a friend's consulting positioning doc. This needs refinement but here's the skeleton:

**Core offer:**
I build clean, well-designed websites for founders, independent professionals, and small businesses — designed and launched in two weeks, at a fixed cost, with a clear deliverable from day one.

**Ideal client:**
- Sole proprietors, fractional consultants, independent makers, local businesses, early-stage founders
- Has something real to say about their work but no good place to say it
- Wants to move fast without a long agency process
- Budget: $8K–$14K flat

**Red flags:**
- Needs e-commerce infrastructure
- No clear sense of what they do or who they serve
- Wants unlimited revision rounds

**Differentiators (to sharpen):**
- 15 years of product design at Fidelity, Accenture, Motional — not templates, actual design thinking
- Fixed scope / fixed cost / fixed timeline — the anti-agency model
- One person, start to finish — no account manager, no hand-offs
- Built to work, not just look good

**Proof points (the gap to close):**
None yet. Getting 2–3 early clients (at a deal if needed) and shipping their sites is the immediate priority.

---

## The Plan
1. **Do 2–3 early-client deals** at reduced rate to build proof points and sharpen the process
2. **Nail the positioning statement** — particularly differentiators and who exactly this is for
3. **Build the service site** — separate URL, separate brand identity (may just be "Dan Murphy" but in a different context)
4. **Use danmurphy.work portfolio as the credibility link** — portfolio proves the service, service links back to portfolio

---

## Site / Domain
- Not on danmurphy.work (creates confusion for both audiences)
- Not a hidden slug like /site-build (feels orphaned, looks broken)
- Separate domain — either product-named (`twoweeksite.com` or similar) or person-named (Dan Murphy, different framing)
- Decision on brand identity is part of the positioning work

---

## Open Questions for the New Task

- Which proof-point clients to approach first, and at what rate?
- What does the full positioning statement say once differentiators are tightened?
- What does the site itself look like — one-pager or multi-page?
